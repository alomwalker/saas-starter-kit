# How the kit handles refunds

A buyer who asks for their money back is a support conversation, not a
code path — but the kit is built so that conversation has a clear answer
and the access ends cleanly afterwards. This post covers the promise, the
request, and what happens in the database for each payment provider.

## The promise: 14 days, no questions asked

The license (`LICENSE.md`, § Refunds) gives every buyer a **14-day
money-back guarantee**: a full refund within 14 days of purchase, no
questions asked. The same promise is on the landing page's FAQ and on the
pricing page, next to the prices — $29 Starter and $49 Pro, both
one-time purchases.

After 14 days, refunds are given only if the downloaded files are corrupt
or materially different from what was described. Support — including
refund requests — is by email at alomwalker21@gmail.com, for installation
and setup questions for 30 days after purchase.

## How a buyer asks

The buyer emails support at alomwalker21@gmail.com with the order
details, and the money is returned in full. There is no self-serve refund
button in the dashboard: a refund is the seller's decision, and the kit
keeps it that way on purpose — the dashboard's billing page links to the
checkout providers, it does not move money.

## What happens to the buyer's access

The kit is a one-time purchase, so there is no subscription to cancel in
the usual sense — but a refunded buyer should not keep a paid plan. That
is handled differently per provider, because the providers report refunds
differently:

- **Stripe.** The Stripe webhook (`src/app/api/webhooks/stripe/route.ts`)
  listens for `charge.refunded` and marks the buyer's subscription
  cancelled, which downgrades them to the free plan. A Stripe refund
  issued from the Stripe dashboard therefore takes effect automatically
  the moment Stripe sends the event — no manual step in the kit.
- **Polar.** Polar is the merchant of record, so the refund itself is
  issued in Polar's dashboard. The Polar webhook
  (`src/app/api/webhooks/polar/route.ts`) handles `subscription.canceled`
  and `subscription.revoked` by marking the subscription cancelled or
  revoked, which downgrades the buyer to free. So after refunding in
  Polar, cancel or revoke the buyer's order/subscription there and the
  kit follows.

In both cases the downgrade is the same one the cancellation paths use —
the buyer's `User.plan` goes back to `free` and the dashboard shows the
free plan. Nothing is deleted: the user, their customers and their data
stay, only the paid plan is removed.

## What the seller should check

1. The refund was actually issued in Polar or Stripe — the kit never
   moves money, it only reacts to the provider's events.
2. The webhook is reachable and its secret is set (`POLAR_WEBHOOK_SECRET`
   or `STRIPE_WEBHOOK_SECRET`) — otherwise the downgrade event never
   arrives and the buyer keeps the paid plan until the seller changes it
   by hand.
3. The buyer got a reply at the support email. The guarantee promises a
   full refund within 14 days; the email is the record that it happened.
