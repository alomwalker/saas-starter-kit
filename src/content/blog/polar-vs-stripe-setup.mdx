The kit ships with two ways to take money, because buyers live in different places and the payment rails do not treat them equally. You pick one on the pricing page — Polar is the default — and the buyer sees the same plans either way.

## Polar: the merchant of record

Polar is the legal seller. It handles VAT and sales tax, takes the payment, and pays you out. That is the whole point of a merchant of record: you never touch card details and never register for tax in the buyer's country.

What you need:

1. A Polar account and one product per plan (Starter and Pro).
2. The product IDs in `POLAR_PRODUCT_ID_STARTER` / `POLAR_PRODUCT_ID_PRO`.
3. An access token in `POLAR_ACCESS_TOKEN`.
4. A webhook pointed at `/api/webhooks/polar` with `POLAR_WEBHOOK_SECRET`.

Polar charges 5% + $0.50 per sale. Payouts run through Stripe Connect, so Polar will ask for identity verification and a bank account before it can accept money — a payout account is a prerequisite to going live, not an afterthought.

## Stripe: direct checkout

Stripe checkout is the familiar card form. The kit creates the checkout session with inline `price_data`, so you do **not** have to pre-create products in Stripe — one secret key is enough.

What you need:

1. `STRIPE_SECRET_KEY`.
2. A webhook pointed at `/api/webhooks/stripe` with `STRIPE_WEBHOOK_SECRET`.

The webhook verifies the signature (HMAC-SHA256) and activates the plan from the session's metadata. A refunded charge downgrades the buyer to free.

## Which one should you pick?

- **You cannot use Stripe as a seller in your country** (Stripe does not onboard sellers everywhere — Bangladesh, for example, is not supported for receiving). Then Polar is the answer: it is the merchant of record and pays out where Stripe will not.
- **Your buyers expect a card form and you already have Stripe.** Use Stripe — it is one key and no product setup.
- **You want both.** Ship both. The pricing page already has the toggle, and each webhook activates plans independently.

## How plan activation works

Both webhooks do the same thing in the end: match the payment to a plan, find the buyer by email, and upsert their `Subscription` row. A cancellation or refund marks it cancelled and drops the buyer back to the free plan. Unknown events and unknown product IDs are acknowledged and ignored, never crashed on.
